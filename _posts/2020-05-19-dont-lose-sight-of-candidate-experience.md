As the number of unemployed individuals continues to rise, so to will, the number of individuals who will keep applying to open positions. Over the last few years, we’ve seen increasing popularity of company review boards such as Glassdoor and Indeed, these sites make it easier for candidates to tell about their experience interviewing at a company. Candidates are looking for a transparent process and want to know what to expect. Be rational and empathetic towards your candidates. During a pandemic or during good economic times we should work towards providing a great candidate experience. We know that times will improve and if that candidate may not be a fit now, you may want them to interview during good economic times. If candidates are treated poorly now but still receive an offer, once the economy picks up they will leave causing more turn over for your organization that could have been avoided.

A few reminders:

Post real jobs! One of the most frustrating parts of a job search is applying to a position. If the company doesn’t believe the role will be filled, it should be closed. Don’t leave it open to collect the resumes. Candidates want to see openings that are going to be filled. They don’t want to waste their time applying to openings that won’t be filled.

Interviewing is getting tough due to the pandemic. We are on video calls all the time and now video calls are being the norm for interviews. As states begin to open up be cautious that some candidates may not want to come in for an interview. Rather have a video interview because of anxiety due to the fear of catching COVID-19. It’s ok that the hiring manager may not have the same anxiety but we should empathize with those that do. Not adding to the stress of interviewing, candidates should not have to face the stress of interviewing and the fear of COVID.

Don’t ghost candidates! Now more than ever keep your candidates informed. If you are looking to fill a role in six months, tell your candidate. Don’t let your candidate find out the hard way - by not telling them. Keep them informed in the process and if you aren’t moving forward with a candidate.

At the end of the day, have empathy towards your candidates. Some candidates are going through a tough time. Some candidates may be trying to look for a new position. Some candidates may want to see what else is out there. Think through your recruitment process and make sure it’s the same process you would go through if you were in a tough situation. If not, revise the process until you get it right.
