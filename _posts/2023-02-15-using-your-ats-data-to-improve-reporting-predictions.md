Organizations often report only the bare minimum information from recruitment data, even though there is always more data that could be reported to identify issues, solutions, and successes. This incomplete reporting often leads to a lack of full understanding about the recruitment team's performance. Your Applicant Tracking System (ATS) has a plethora of data that can help companies improve their recruitment processes and stay ahead of the competition. Here are a few ways in which companies can leverage this data:

- Targeted Job Advertising: By analyzing data on previous job applicants and hires, companies can determine the channels and platforms that attract the right type of candidates. This information can then be used to create targeted job advertising campaigns to reach potential candidates where they are most active online. Instead of looking at the source of each hire, explore the source per application or where your highest performers (using your performance review process) came from to best understand where your best hires came from.
- Improved Candidate Screening: The data collected in an ATS can be used to quickly and accurately screen candidates. The typical time to fill or time to hire metric is often given as a sign of success. However, how about reviewing the time from application to initial contact, or measuring how long the interview process takes?
- Increased Diversity: The data collected in an ATS can also be used to increase diversity in the workplace. For example, by tracking the number of applicants from different demographic groups, a company can determine if it is attracting a diverse pool of candidates. If not, the company can make changes to its recruitment process to increase diversity and inclusiveness.
- Understanding Trends: Most ATS systems have a resume parsing tool that enables tracking and trending of candidate application sources and job search trends. Take for example you have three positions open in Kansas City and each position has 20 applications, but you notice 10 applications are from the same company or university. You can start tracking trends of candidates coming from other companies.

An ATS has plenty of information - be creative in reviewing ATS data. These are just a few ways to start understanding a recruitment process, but there are a lot more ways to review data and understand inefficiencies and successes.
