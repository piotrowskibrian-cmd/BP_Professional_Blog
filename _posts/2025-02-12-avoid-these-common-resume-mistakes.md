Your resume is not meant to get you a job—it’s meant to land you an interview. Make sure you focus on showing your best self on your resume to capture the attention of the hiring manager and recruiter. Being in recruiting as long as I have - these are the most common mistakes I see on resumes.

1\. Poor Formatting and Lack of White Space

A messy, hard-to-read resume can send your application straight to the rejection. Make sure the font is easy to read and maintain spacing and margins. Your resume has less than 30 seconds to capture the attention of the recruiter - make sure it's easy to skim.

2\. Keep Your Most Recent Experience Towards the Top and Clearly Display Your Title, Company Name, and Accomplishments

Your most recent and relevant experience should be the first thing a recruiter sees. Unless you’re just starting your career, place your education at the bottom and include a professional summary at the top. List your most recent jobs first, in reverse chronological order, ensuring that key achievements are easy to spot. I have even seen resumes where achievements are called out as the first couple bullet points.  Make sure your job title, employer, and employment dates are easy to find—if recruiters can’t locate them quickly, they may move on.

3\. Grammar and Spelling Mistakes

Poor grammar and spelling errors make a resume look unprofessional and careless, often leading to immediate rejection. Always proofread your resume multiple times. Don't be afraid to use ChatGPT or Grammarly to identify and correct grammar issues.

4\. Not Adding Achievements

Too many people just add day-to-day responsibilities and leave out achievements. Hiring managers want to see the impact you made, not just a rundown of daily tasks. Use metrics and results to showcase accomplishments. Instead of writing *“Managed a sales team,”* try *“Led a sales team of 10, increasing revenue by 25% in six months.”* Numbers help illustrate your success.

5\. Resume Should Not Be More Than Two Pages

A resume that is too long can lose the recruiter’s attention, while one that is too short may not provide enough detail.

6\. If You Don’t Work for a Household Brand, Add a Summary of the Company

If your previous employer isn’t widely known, recruiters may not understand the significance of your experience. Provide a brief company description under the company name to give context, such as industry, size, and market position.

7\. Have Someone Review Your Resume

It may seem simple, but having someone you trust review your resume. A fresh set of eyes can help catch errors and ensure your resume clearly communicates your experience. Ask someone to skim your resume and see if they can quickly understand your background. If they take a deeper look, they should also be able to recognize your key accomplishments.

Your resume should make it easy for hiring managers and recruiters to see why you are a great fit. Keep it clean, simple, and easy to read, and you’ll increase your chances of landing an interview. I'll talk next time about how to use ChatGPT to enhance your resume.
