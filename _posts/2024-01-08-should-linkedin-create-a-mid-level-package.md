In the world of talent acquisition, LinkedIn has established itself as an indispensable tool for recruiters globally. Yet, there's a noticeable gap in its service spectrum, particularly between the Recruiter Lite and LinkedIn Recruiter offerings. The significant price gap – with Recruiter Lite around $1,200 per seat per year and LinkedIn Recruiter surpassing $15,000 per seat per year – highlights the need for a middle-tier option that could benefit more companies. Clearly, there is a reason why a package doesn't exist but there is a need.

LinkedIn dominates the recruitment market, with many companies relying on its services in some capacity. The primary distinctions between the two packages are price, the number of InMails, and search access. Recruiter Lite allows searching up to 3rd-degree connections and sending 30 InMails per month. In contrast, LinkedIn Recruiter offers nearly unlimited search capabilities across LinkedIn, 150 InMails per month, and additional insights such as "Open to Work" and "Likely to Respond". It also includes ATS integration and tools for collaboration and efficiency.

Despite these offerings, a significant segment of the market – particularly medium-sized businesses and startups – does not require the full spectrum of LinkedIn Recruiter but needs more than what Recruiter Lite offers. This gap indicates a missed opportunity for LinkedIn.

A mid-tier package, priced between $5,000 to $8,000 annually, would address this gap. It could appeal to hiring managers and recruitment teams in larger enterprises as well as smaller businesses. A package at the mid-tier could include:

- InMail Capacity: 70-100 messages per month, offering more flexibility than Recruiter Lite but not as extensive as LinkedIn Recruiter.
- Network Access: Broader access than Recruiter Lite, though not encompassing the entire LinkedIn network.
- Selective Feature Set: Maintaining core functionalities like advanced search filters and basic collaboration tools, while possibly omitting features like ATS integration to manage costs.

Introducing a mid-level LinkedIn recruitment package would fill a void but I can guarantee until LinkedIn loses money a change won't happen. However, creating a package like this would be very helpful to recruiters at smaller companies or recruiters who don't need to reach out to as many people monthly.
