Artificial intelligence has the opportunity to support recruitment in ways we can’t imagine but could have unwanted consequences. Google has created Ai that was used to call a restaurant to make dinner reservations. However, Amazon created Ai to support recruitment but the Ai started discriminating against applicants. The capability of Ai is growing and the investments in recruitment tech are growing every year. Already, we have Ai that helps us daily but what if we take Ai a step further? What if Ai can help us transform the quickness and services we offer as a Talent Acquisitions team?

Minus the obvious possibilities of scheduling interview, what if Ai can do more? Recruiters spend less than a minute reviewing a resume and Ai already can screen resumes matching your resume to what the company actually needs. However, what if your ATS would match a candidate to the job then schedule time with the recruiter to do their prescreen interviews. Hopefully, Ai could get to the part that it can scan the candidates resumes, learn what you really need and give you questions that you should be asking in the interview. As humans we are creatures of habitat, we all have a list of questions we want to ask the candidates. This would help us get more substance out of our interviews.

On the side of sourcing, in today’s market, it’s probably where most of our time is spent. Ai could be a bot that is tased to use X-ray techniques to find more candidates through different means. Now taking it a step further, what if the bot sends the first message to the candidates with a quick introduction to your company, the position, and the first pass with the candidate. If the individual is interested, then your recruiter could get on the phone and have those relationship-building conversations.

Freeing up recruiters time to do more relationship building with candidates and clients would allow recruiters to give more value add to their companies. Really learning what clients want and need in the requisitions they have open. Also, recruiters will be able to spend more time with candidates. It also reduced the burden of administrative tasks that recruiters are assigned.

There are two real concerns of Ai; first, if companies don’t set any checks and balances with their Ai. In the case of Amazon, Amazon realized their system was biased and acted accordingly to find a solution. Now, how long and how quickly they acted is another debate but you'll need to always check your metrics to make sure it's still a relevant tool for your company. The second is implementing an Ai without a real strategy to leverage the tech. Without knowing how this solution will support your organization, your clients, and your recruitment team. For example, we could implement chatbots into most Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS). However, if you don’t know why you are implementing it, what problems you are trying to solve, or if you don’t have the technical support to properly implement; you may want to rethink why you are implementing that Ai. Broken or not functioning Ai will cause more issues with your recruitment team and with your managers than most exempt. It will also strain relationships with vendors, support staff, and recruitment teams.

There is no question that Ai in recruitment is here and will continue to grow. There will be people that resist the change but there will be others that embrace it. Not all Ai will be right for you and your organization. Really understand what the needs are, how it will support your organization, make sure there are clear expectations and the technical support to add the tech. There really is no telling how much Ai will help but it’s important to keep an open mind.
