With the increase of fake news in today's world, bias is everywhere. However, bias has increased to more than racial, religion, gender, or sexual orientation. We are now facing bias with what universities someone attended, where they work if they job-hopped, foreign work experience, and the list can go on.

There are easy ways to remove bias by implementing technology to support removing anything in a resume that can be viewed as bias. The major flaw of that technology is when that person sits across from the hiring manager, they will see their race, gender, or whatever the software is trying to hide. Software is expected to learn from it's human creator. If the software learns a pattern of bias it will promote bias behavior. The team that manages the ATS and hiring reporting will have to audit the hiring process to ensure the software isn’t creating or supporting bias.

Over the last few years, the industry has noticed a bias in all shapes and forms. Managers won’t interview candidates that job-hopped because they don’t know how long those individuals will stay. Regardless of why the candidates left their positions. If an entry-level candidate does come from the right university or college managers may not consider the candidate because the candidate didn’t graduate from the “right” university. There is countless other bias that could be explained.

What is the best way to manage bias? The answer to this train your recruitment team to deal with bias. If the recruitment team knows how to deal with bias, they can educate managers of bias. It’s by education, as a recruitment professional, there is a hope that recruiters want to fill the role with the best candidate on the market. Regardless of the candidates' background, recruiters need to educate the manager why this candidate is a fit for the position.

Why does it start with recruitment? The recruiter needs to take ownership of all steps of the recruitment process. If the recruiter senses any bias, they need to address the hiring manager as they are partnering with that manager to fill the role of the organization. If the hiring manager still doesn’t see an issue, then addressing it with the hiring manager's manager or HRBPs. At the end of the day, recruitment wants to fill the role with the best candidate. Managers may have unconscience bias that the manager needs to be educated on this bias.

As recruitment works to fill requisitions with the best candidate education is sometimes necessary about the market and any issues in the recruitment process. Meaning if the best candidate didn’t come from the best university or does not come from a top player in the industry, recruiters need to convince the manager to talk to the candidate. Recruiters should call the hiring manager and address the issue head-on. It’s the easiest way to have the hiring manager to at least speak with the candidate. Explain to the manager why that candidate is a fit and at least everything is out in the open. If the manager says no, then get other people involved in the organization.

Addressing hiring bias is never fun but recruitment needs to be willing to address issues. Recruiters know the market and hopefully can convince the hiring manager to at least speak with the candidate.
