Welcome to our new normal; social distancing, virtual meetings, and virtual happy hours. Employees that could, started working from home last week. This week a quarter of the U.S. population is under a shelter in place. If you were like me, I had to use my cell phone as a hot spot for a few hours due to my home internet provider internet experiencing an outage. Working parents started letting their kids jump onto video calls, dogs barking in the background and we all adapted. The fortunate companies still need to hire people. Hopefully, the people that are out of work can find their way to industries that need staff. As we move forward in recruiting what changes will we see in the coming days, weeks, and months?

Candidates that are currently employed just became more difficult to recruit. During times of economic uncertainty, candidates would rather stay with the devil they know versus the devil they don't know. For the requisitions, that need to be filled immediately, you’ll need to show candidates that your organization is stable. That their role within your organization is valued, it’s hard to prove but showing how your company is prepared, what they would be working on and why they would be valuable in your organization.

In several months we will hopefully be passed this situation and we will be rebuilding. We will have requisitions to fill. For those candidates, that aren’t interested in changing at this moment, start pipelining. The companies that are ready to hire quickly and be ready to hire the best candidate on the market will climb out quicker. You’ll hire the best people that can help you grow your team, department, and even your company.

For projects where you need staff temporary for an implementation, an initiative, or for a short term project; be honest with your candidate pool. Don’t string that candidate along, be upfront with duration. We have changed how we have recruited within the gig economy already. There is a large candidate pool that is there willing to work on short-term contracts if you are upfront. The honesty will build greater trust between the candidate and the recruiters. Not to mention the trust it begins to build between your organization and the candidate.

As we practice social distancing onboarding is going to become harder. In most cases, we will need someone to prepare a laptop for a new hire. That laptop then needs to be mailed to the new hire. Fortunately, the federal government has loosened guidelines on I-9 verification so new hires do not have to come into the office. We won’t get into the challenges of onboarding remote staff but we may leave that for another time

Above may sound easy but it won’t be. You’ll be met with doubts and pushback from candidates, peers, managers, and/or executives but, it’s important to tell your side of building relationships early. We want to build relationships for the right reasons.
