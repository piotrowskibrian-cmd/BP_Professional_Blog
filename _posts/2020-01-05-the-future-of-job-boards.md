Job boards as we know them today haven’t always been around; we use to call them the “classified” section of the Newspaper. As we know job boards today - where you go to a website to find open positions have been around since the dot com boom. For the sake of this conversation, we will call them “Original Job Boards.” There isn’t anything special about these sites. Companies are required to pay a fee to post the openings and candidates go to the site to search for open jobs. Other job boards have come to the market to builds sites that aggregate from company career sites. Meaning the job board comes scraps all the open positions from the companies website to post on their job board. Original Job Boards and the Aggregator sites are both still around in today’s market. The major difference between the two styles of websites; for the original job boards companies need to pay to be on those sites and aggregator sites you'll need to pass along the URL of your career site to the job board.

In today’s market and with current tech, candidates want ease while applying. Think of the last time you may have applied for a job. If the application process wasn’t quick, easy, and straightforward; you probably stopped applying in the middle of the application. Remeber Google became popular because they wanted their site to be simple. Companies when posting on a job board, want the same but they also want it to be cheap. There are integrations that you can make with your ATS (application tracking system) to post your openings on job boards. However, these integrations cost money that most T.A. teams don’t have and are time-consuming. This is where the aggregator site has an advantage. They don’t charge you to post on their sites and they are free to post your jobs. Aggregator sites make money in other ways and we won't discuss that in this post.

If you had the option between sites, you’d probably pick the Aggregator sites to post your open position. That means most others would probably post their open positions on the Aggregated site. For active candidates who are applying to many postings, many will probably notice that the aggregators have more content, remember we want it to be quick, easy, and straightforward. Aggregated sites are quicker because there is more content and job seekers don't need to go to several sites to search. They should be pretty easy to use because you can search everything at once. Straightforward meaning their user-interface (UI) is simple to understand. Therefore, they will probably spend more time searching for new jobs on aggregated sites because there is more content.

The original pay per post boards still have a place in the market but I don’t see them as the future. The technology is dated and still relatively similar to how they launched years ago. They will need to catch up and start scrapping corporate sites to keep up with the quick, easy and straightforward. I do believe that the future of job boards lies in sites that are aggregated based. Think of search engines, we start most questions in one location. Job Aggregated career sites have the most content in one place. If you are looking for an accounting position, you can type an accountant in the search and thousands of results will populate. While the original job boards may still have thousands of postings but fewer companies are willing to post because they are required to pay to post their opening. In today's world where content means everything, aggregated sites have so many more job ads. Much like how classified ads have died off; if job boards don't keep progressing they will be replaced.
