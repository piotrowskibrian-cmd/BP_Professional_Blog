Within days the economy went from full employment to a record-setting amount of people filing for unemployment benefits. In other words, we went from having more jobs open than people that are qualified to more people that are jobs available. Many millions of people are now looking for work that didn’t plan to be looking. People are updating resumes and applying for jobs quickly. It may seem difficult to find work now and it may seem difficult to get noticed. How do you get noticed in this tough economic time and when your resume is skimmed quickly?

If you don’t have a current resume, start writing one. Your resume will not be perfect after the first time you write it. Everyday edit your resume until you are ready to start sharing it. A few quick tips, clearly outline your dates of employment, title, and company you worked for or still work at. While writing out your duties and responsibilities make sure you are adding your accomplishments while you were in your role.

It’s true, your resume will have roughly one minute to be considered. Keep all the important responsibilities and accomplishments near the top. Also, make sure to use buzzwords or industry terms near the top of each position you’ve held. Yes, some companies use software that reads your resume and matches it to their descriptions. It is important to use buzzwords and speak about what the job description writes. If the job description states you’ll be creating KPI’s, explain how you’ve done that in a prior position. Your resume is only there to get a phone interview with the company so your next employer wants to see your accomplishments.

Make sure you have white space on your resume. Don’t decrease your margins or lower your font when you are writing your resume. It may reduce your page count but it will be harder to read. Keeping margins won’t clutter your resume and will make it easier to read. Once your resume crosses two and a half pages nothing more will be read by the recruiter or hiring manager. Having too long of a resume isn't a bad thing, it's just that interest will be lost and the work you put in will not be read. Keep the important bullets on the resume and get rid of the rest of the bullet points.

Once you submit your resume to a company's applicant tracking system, start networking with people at that company. Even if they don't have a position open and it's a company you want to work for, start networking with members of that company. In the age of physical distancing, use LinkedIn to help you find the recruiter or hiring manager and use those platforms reach out to them. Just send that manager or recruiter an invite to connect and once connected send them a message. Don't be overzealous and send too many messages. Too many messages to managers can come off as annoying or desperate and would disqualify you. The thought process is that how you act as a candidate; you'll act like that as an employee. Use good judgment here.

There is no one-size-fits-all solution to getting your resume noticed. The above should not be your only option, there are many ways to get yourself noticed. Be creative and let your personality shine but, don't be annoying. It’s ok to follow up but following up in 48 hours is too quick.
