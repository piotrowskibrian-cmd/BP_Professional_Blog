President Trump has secured a second term in office. Given his campaign promises and the policies of his first term, we can expect some significant changes on the horizon. Here are my top five changes to watch for in his second term:

1\. Tighter Restrictions on Visas and Immigration

Trump’s first term saw a crackdown on immigration, and a second term could further intensify these policies. His promise of mass deportations would likely lead to a reduced labor pool, especially in industries that rely on migrant workers, such as agriculture and the trades. Talent Acquisition teams may need to adjust by focusing more on domestic recruitment and adjusting strategies for the tightening of visa processes.

2\. Reduction in DEI Mandates

Companies could face fewer requirements to track or report diversity metrics  (EEO) in a second Trump term. This would allow talent acquisition teams to shift away from federally mandated DEI goals and possibly focus more on merit-based hiring practices. There could be fewer resources allocated to diversity initiatives, as Trump’s administration has been critical of federal DEI mandates in the past.

3\. Overtime Eligibility Restrictions

While Trump campaigned on not taxing overtime salaries, a second term could lead to changes in overtime eligibility. Starting January 1, 2025, workers earning $58,656 or more will be eligible for overtime pay, but under Trump’s presidency, we may see this threshold increase. Talent acquisition teams will need to rethink compensation structures and salary classifications, particularly for salaried positions.

Update (11/18/2024): Since the publication of this article, the rule regarding overtime pay based on salary has been struck down by a Texas court.

4\. Healthcare and Benefits Reductions

Trump has been a vocal opponent of the Affordable Care Act (ACA), and a second term could bring efforts to reduce healthcare coverage and employee benefits. Under the ACA, businesses with 50 or more employees are required to offer healthcare coverage. However, if the ACA is repealed, companies with more than 50 employees will no longer be required to provide insurance, which could impact talent acquisition strategies as companies adjust their benefits offerings.

5\. Push for a Return to office

This is a major assumption, but as a real estate developer, Trump has a vested interest in the commercial real estate market. With corporate real estate vacancies still high, I foresee a potential push for workers to return to physical offices. This could limit access to remote talent and put pressure on companies to recruit employees willing to relocate or commute to corporate offices.

A second term under President Trump will likely bring changes that impact our daily lives and the corporate world. However, the extent of these changes remains uncertain until they take effect. The five points I’ve outlined are based on campaign promises and speeches from the campaign trail, but we’ll know more in early 2025 about how these shifts will truly affect the world of talent acquisition.
