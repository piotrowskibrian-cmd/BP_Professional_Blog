How would the interview process look if we rethought our current recruitment process and challenged our current process for applying, interviews, and offers? If we truly made everything about the candidate experience?

I am willing to bet most interviewing process looks like this; post a job description that hasn’t been updated in years, have candidates apply, review resumes, a phone interview, a face-to-face interview, maybe a candidate assessment, and offer that is negotiated. There is nothing unique in the process. What if we changed that?

What if a hiring manager took the time to review the description. Truly write a new or updating the description by partnering with your marketing team to make sure your description is engaging, transparent, and truthful. The application process should be easy, quick, no need to upload a cover letter (because who actually reads it) and the resume parsing feature actually works. Feedback to candidates that aren't moving to candidates is done timely and constructive. How would a candidate react if they receive an email that tells them they don't meet the minimum requirements because they don't hold a bachelor's degree, a CPA, or any other requirements?

Instead of requiring people to come in during business hours to interview, what if we met a candidate before or after work to accommodate their schedule? If you need someone to come in during business hours, what if we compensate the candidate for their time coming in for the interview? For a company to truly care about candidate experience would we put candidates through a ridiculous interview process (panel interviews, stress-interviews, or day-long interviews)?  Do you have candidates participate in dated assessments? What would happen if we got to know the candidates? Figure out what the candidate wants in their next move? We should try to make the candidates feel at home in the first interview and want them to come work for the company. Would we speak to the candidate truthfully about the struggles they will face, what they will like, and what is boring parts are of the job?

The offer process is still negotiated as you would a home with the recruiter acting like your realtor. What would it take to offer candidates what they are expecting and what is in line for market compensation? What would happen if we offered our best offer upfront? Having a candidate excited about an offer and having them work for your company.

We could talk about each of these steps individually and in greater depth. However, I wanted to start the conversation. As T.A. professionals we shouldn't treat candidates as it's a privilege to speak with our companies. We should remember to make the process enjoyable and pleasant.
