Discussing salary expectations has often been a  taboo subject. Both candidates and employers may feel uncomfortable discussing compensation, with the fear that it could harm the interview process somehow. Having open and honest conversations about salary can actually benefit both parties, let's face it we don't want to waste anyone's time. Why should we be transparent in our process?

1\. Clarifies Expectations: All parties in an interview process often worry about asking about salary, as they don’t want to appear greedy, cheap, or unprofessional. However, discussing salary upfront can help to clarify expectations and prevent disappointment later on. The sooner all parties know the salary expectations the sooner you will know if the range is correct. It's also sooner you know if you are wasting time.

2\. Avoids Misaligned Expectations: If a candidate and an employer have different expectations about salary, it can create tension and lead to disappointment. By openly discussing salary during an interview, both parties can ensure they are on the same page and avoid misaligned expectations.

3\. Improves the Negotiating Process: If a candidate knows what the company is offering, they can use that information to negotiate for a better salary. Open salary discussions can lead to more effective negotiations, where both parties can reach a mutually acceptable agreement.

4\. Increases Transparency: Open salary discussions can increase transparency and help to build trust between candidates and companies. Companies that are transparent about compensation are more likely to be perceived as fair and trustworthy.

While both candidates and employers may have reservations about openly discussing compensation, doing so can benefit both parties. It can help clarify expectations, prevent misaligned expectations, improve the negotiating process, and increase transparency and trust. By being transparent in our process and having open and honest conversations about salary, we can create a more fair and equitable job market where all parties are informed and empowered to make informed decisions. Ultimately, it is in everyone's best interest to have a more open and honest approach to discussing compensation.
