The evenings are cooling down, college football is back, and employers are showing up on campus looking for next year’s interns.

If you’re anything like I was, this time of year felt… awkward. You can probably thank a stranger danger or just being shy. Personally, I hated walking up to employers and starting small talk. Talking to them about internships possible full time employment etc.

Now? I am that person you’re walking up to and talking about working. This is the advice and knowledge I wish I had when I was an undergrad. So here’s my guide to help you get through it and maybe even enjoy it.

**1. Have Your Career Center Review Your Resume**

Before you do anything else, clean up your resume.

Most schools have a career center that will review your resume. Even if it’s not perfect, make sure it’s clear, easy to skim, and highlights internship-ready experience. Think: class projects, campus orgs, part-time work, and transferable skills.

Bring multiple copies, both printed and digital.

It’s a 50/50 split on who wants paper vs. a PDF upload or QR scan.

**2. Do Your Research (and Ask Smart Questions)**

Find out which companies are attending, both in person and virtual. Look them up. Check what roles they’re hiring for on Handshake or other platforms. Pay attention to whether they offer internships, co-ops, or entry-level positions in your major.

Then when you show up, ask directly:

> “Are you hiring interns in \[your major\] this summer / fall / winter?”
>
> “What do you usually look for in internship candidates?”

Most people at booths love direct questions. It's to the point and direct. It shows you’re prepared, intentional, and not just there for the free pens.

**3. Dress the Part**

Professional doesn’t have to mean uncomfortable or outdated.

You can skip the full suit unless you’re into it, tell them Brian said it's ok.

- Men: Polo or button-down, clean pants or slacks.
- Women: Blouse or nice top, slacks or a skirt.

**Please** don’t show up in gym clothes or… overalls. Yes, that actually happened. Memorable? Sure. But not in a good way.

**4. Practice Your Pitch (Yes, Out Loud)**

You don’t need to memorize a monologue. But you do need to know how to introduce yourself.

Pro tip: start with companies you’re less interested in.

It’s a low-pressure way to warm up. I wish I had done this because it would have helped calm nerves and helped where I stumbled in my pitch.

Companies only unless they are super small send people that are easy to talk to, so use those early conversations to shake off the nerves before you head to the companies you’re really excited about.

**5. Track Who You Talked To**

You’ll forget. Trust me. Especially after 10+ booths.

Take a moment after each convo to jot down; Company name, Who you spoke with, What you talked about, and next steps.

This makes your follow-up way more personal, and way more likely to be remembered.

**6. Follow Up**

If someone gives you a name or business card, follow up

Not with a copy-paste “thanks for your time” reference something you actually discussed:

> “I appreciated your insight on what you look for in intern candidates. I looked into the marketing ops role you mentioned and would love to stay in touch as I explore next steps. Thank you for the insights on Professor Smith.”
>
> They meet a lot of students. Make it easy to remember you.

**7. Freshmen & Sophomores, You Belong Too**

Even if companies aren’t hiring your year yet, career fairs are still worth showing up to.

- Walk the floor
- Get comfortable approaching recruiters.
- Ask questions like:

> “I know I’m early, but what do you look for in a strong intern resume?”
>
> “What skills help students stand out?”

Starting early builds confidence *and* connections that can pay off down the road.

### Bonus Tips

- Don’t roam the fair in a pack. You’re not in high school.
- Don’t lead with “I need a job.” Lead with curiosity.
- Take the free swag. Seriously. We don’t want to carry it home.

Want to stand out? Show up prepared, be curious, and leave a positive impression. That’s it. You’ve got this.
