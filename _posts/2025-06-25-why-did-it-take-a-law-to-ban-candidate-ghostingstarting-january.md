Starting January 1, 2026, Ontario is officially banning candidate ghosting.

Under the Working for Workers Five Act, also known as Bill 190, employers will be legally required to follow up with any candidate they interview for a publicly posted role within 45 days.

It sounds like common sense. But if you’ve worked in recruiting, you know how often it happens. The hiring manager goes quiet. The role gets paused. The recruiter forgets to circle back. The ATS doesn’t send reminders. It’s not always intentional, but it leaves the candidate hanging.

And now, that silence becomes non-compliant.

The law also requires pay ranges to be included in job postings. Employers can no longer list "Canadian experience required." If AI is used at any point in the hiring process, that has to be disclosed too, thanks to Bill 149. On top of it all, companies need to keep records of the entire process for three years.

Candidate experience isn’t just a best practice anymore. It’s the law.

This is going to challenge a lot of teams. Most processes weren’t built for this kind of structure or accountability. Someone will claim a quick coffee chat wasn’t a real interview. Others will just miss the window. Some will try to do the bare minimum and call it compliance.

But maybe this is the kind of pressure we need.

Because it shouldn’t take a law to tell someone they didn’t get the job.

Always remember we are in the people business and we always do better.
