Not all trends and fads in recruitment should be used. This past week I saw an article in the Wall Street Journal about a new interview technique called Cluster Hiring. Recruitment and Talent Acquisitions is one of the only professions, that loves to jump to the next trend with very little research or case studies. Trends that are specific to interviewing and candidate selection should be approached with caution. Anyone who is involved in hiring has come across these trends when involved in the recruitment process. In my professional career, hiring, (strangely enough) has always been the most difficult decision I have to make. The industry tries to make it easier with creating new strategies for candidate selection.

Cluster hiring, from my understanding, is when you have a group of people and you split them up into smaller groups. For this example let’s say we have nine people that come in for an interview. We split them into three groups of three and we assign them all a project that will take the same amount of time for all groups to complete. These three groups of three have the same project and the group that does the best on the project all will receive a job offer.

There are many interview fads that we could talk about including panel interviews, stress interviews, interviews conducted only through text messages, etc. Companies try to adopt new practices because hiring is difficult, expensive, and time-consuming. It takes time to prep for interviews, conducting an interview, debriefing with the interview team and to negotiate offices. You also have to pay for recruiters, salaries of staff involved in the interview, ATS, background checks, etc. The less time we all feel we dedicate to interviewing the more we feel we can accomplish in our daily responsibilities. Following these trends may give us a false feeling we flushed out something in the interview while we may have spent the entire interview focusing on the negative.

From what I know about Cluster Hiring, it may work well for consulting companies or companies that hire classes of people. However, for companies that will use this strategy, they will miss out on stronger candidates that surrounded by candidates that don't work well in groups. On the opposite side, you may hire a group that works really well for that project. When they are hired and assigned new project teams they may not work well on other projects. This strategy focuses not on what the candidate's prior experience and accomplishments are but rather on their one accomplishment.

Whatever trends that are followed to interview candidates, it's our responsibility to make sure there are case studies to support your decision. We have been using behavioral and situational interviewing for years. There is plenty of data and evidence to support using these techniques. We have to make sure we are getting to know the candidate’s professional experience, skillset, personality, strengths, and weaknesses. Getting to know this information will help you to make better hiring decisions and knowing if the candidate will fit in.
