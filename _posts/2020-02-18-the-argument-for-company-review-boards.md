In today’s society, everyone is getting rated. We all have credit scores, our Uber drivers rate us, we rate our Amazon purchases, so why shouldn’t we rate our employers?

How do these review boards work? Simple! Companies like Glassdoor and Indeed have been asking their users to rate current and past employers. If you worked for a company you can review the company on compensation, benefits, management, culture, day-to-day life etc. Glassdoor and Indeed have their own algorithms to rank the company (normally on a 5 point scale).

This market has been growing it’s only a matter of time before Google and other companies will jump on this bandwagon too. Just a short time ago, I was under the impression that these sites would fail or rebrand to a job board. I was wrong and they have become more of a destination for candidates looking for a new position.

Image, you are approached by a recruiter to work for a company. If you are interested in the position the recruiter reached out to you about, you probably will try to find out as much information about that company as possible. Most companies are not Google, LinkedIn, Facebook, Amazon, etc and have instant brand recognition. Therefore, the candidate will try to search for as much information as possible which will probably take them to Glassdoor or Indeed. From there you can see what past and current employees have to say, the good, bad, and ugly comments. The reviews will start giving the candidate a picture of what it’s like to work at that company. With the candidate-driven market that we are in today, candidates have plenty of choices. Why shouldn’t a candidate use the tools at their disposal to make a better employment choice?

The companies that are utilizing review boards correctly will be able to see what issues or what they are doing right quickly. If you notice your developers are all saying management is awful but all other departments are leaving positive reviews, you know where your issues lie in your company. Additionally, if you have an office that has stellar reviews and another office is poor, you can use those reviews to correct any issues. Most companies perform some type of exit interviews. However, you don’t get the information from the employee until they leave the organization and the company is left to replace that employee. The review boards give you the opportunity to engage employees and correct issues before they leave your organization. In theory, improving your retention rates.

Both candidates and companies can gain a lot from these review boards. If utilized in the correct way, there is much to gain from the sites. However, employers and candidates need to use the sites correctly for the sites to be beneficial.
