Many states and major cities have enacted pay transparency laws. These laws require employers to disclose pay rates to potential candidates. They are designed to promote fairness and equality in the workplace by ensuring that all employees are paid fairly for their work, regardless of factors such as gender, race, or other personal characteristics.

One of the key benefits of pay transparency laws is that they help to reduce pay disparities. By requiring employers to disclose their pay rates, these laws make it easier for employees to determine whether they are being paid fairly for their work. This can help to eliminate pay gaps, which can be a major source of unfairness and inequality in the workplace.

Companies that post a salary range in their job descriptions already see an increase in applications. For the Zoom generation, in particular, not including a salary range in the job ad often leads to fewer applications. Recently, a TikToker saw a job posting and applied because the salary range was above her current salary. Stories like this support the need for pay transparency.

Pay transparency laws can help to attract and retain top talent. When potential employees know that an employer is paying top wages, they are more likely to be interested in working for that employer. This can help the employer to attract and retain the best and brightest workers, which can be a major competitive advantage in today's job market. However, there are fundamental flaws in the laws that make it difficult. For example, a company may post a range from $1 - $1,000,000, which does not fulfill the spirit of the law. We will discuss these issues in a future post.
