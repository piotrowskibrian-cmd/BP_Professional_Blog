Your internship is coming to an end and your next seminar is about to begin. Remember the point of your internship is to gain real world experience. That also means taking initiative and leaning what is next. You'll likely start receiving your good-bye messages from your manager, the company survey about your internship, and all the off-boarding process from HR. However, there are things you can be doing to set yourself up for success for next summer's internship or your first full time offer post graduation.

1\. Don’t Just Clock Out and Ghost.

Send or say thank you note to your manager and any mentors for the opportunity to learn from them. In that conversation don't forget to ask for feedback - it can be tough but if you don't ask you will never know. Make sure you ask them for a recommendation or referral.

2\. Want a Offer for Next summer?  Don’t Assume They Know That.

A lot of interns assume, *“If I do a good job, they’ll offer me a role.”* Companies are bureaucratic in nature and often are only looking until the next quarter. Inform your manager you'd love to come back next summer and don't forget to stay in touch.

3\. Didn’t Get a Return Offer? Here’s How to Handle It.

You’re not alone. Sometimes it’s performance-related, budget, or headcount. Make sure you focus on what you gained: skills, experience, context. Don't forget to ask for feedback. If they’re willing to be honest, *listen. Update your resume and move on to the* next thing. One internship isn’t your entire story.

4\. Stay Top-of-Mind.

Just because your internship ends doesn’t mean the relationship has to. Connect with your team on LinkedIn (now, not in 6 months). Comment and like their posts occasionally, show you’re engaged. Send a quick update email 3–4 months later. Short and sweet:  
“Hope you’re doing well! Just wanted to say thanks again for the internship. I’m graduating in May and still interested in \[company/role\]. Please keep me in mind if anything opens up.”

5\. Still Not Sure What You Want to Do? That’s Normal.

Some of you loved your internship. Some of you are thinking, *“I never want to do this again.”* Either is fine. Internships are about learning, not locking yourself into a career path. Take some time to reflect: What parts of the job did you like? What parts did you dislike drained you? What did you learn about the kind of people or environment you work best in?

The goal isn’t clarity. It’s momentum.
