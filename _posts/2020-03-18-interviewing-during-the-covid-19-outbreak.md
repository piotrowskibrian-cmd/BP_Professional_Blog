Unless you live under a rock, you know COVID-19 has spread across the world. A majority of our workforce is now remote to combat the outbreak. A fair amount of employers still have roles they need to fill, we all need to be flexible and adapt in our current situation.

With most of our workforce working from home, it will be important to stay in communications with our hiring managers. We know things will change daily with our open positions. However, we do have urgent roles that we need to fill. The local & federal government, W.H.O., and many other organizations are recommending social distancing. How will we conduct interviews if we can’t meet a candidate in person? Hopefully, most people will know the answer but we will have to conduct them via video. All of our companies have some types of conferencing systems; Skype, Zoom, GotoMeetings, FaceTime, Etc. Hopefully, you've already made those recommendations to your operations group.

During a video interview, much of the same can be accomplished as a face-to-face interview. A manager can screen the candidate’s technical experience. Managers can screen if the candidate will be a fit with the team. Personality can come off in a video interview and relationships can start. Video interviewing isn't a new fad, it's been around for years but will be used more over the next few weeks.

If a candidate and a manager want to meet face-to-face, it’s important to set some ground rules. It’s very important with Covid-19 if anyone is sick (even with a cold) to postpone the interview. If our local health departments decide to close offices it's is best to either postpone or move the interview to a video interview. As members of recruitment, we do have a duty to protect our staff and we don’t need to feed fear of spreading COVID-19.

Onboarding becomes more important with a remote workforce. Most companies are not prepared to deal with remote onboarding. Most of us if we have a remote employee we ask them to travel to one of our offices to get their computer, tax documents, onboarding documents, and orientations. You’ll need to lean on your I.T. team to prepare laptops and send them to your new hires. If your company can afford to send monitors they will need to send those too. Managers will need to make sure onboarding is conducted remotely and introductions to the team are made virtually. It will be extremely important to start building the relationships with staff remotely and introduce new hires on a conference call with your team or via email.

We are in unprecedented times right now. We need to be adaptable and ready to change with our new reality. Technology can help us get through social distancing. Use technology to support hiring now but don't be afraid to use it after we pass the pandemic.
