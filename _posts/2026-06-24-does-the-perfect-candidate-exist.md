If you’ve spent any time recruiting this year, you’ve probably heard the same thing over and over, “The market is terrible.”

Candidates feel like there aren’t enough opportunities. Hiring managers feel like they aren’t seeing enough qualified talent. Recruiters are working harder to fill positions while searches stay open longer than expected.

The data tells a slightly different story. According to the latest JOLTS report, job openings increased to 7.6 million while employers made 5.1 million hires in April. Companies are still posting jobs. They just aren’t making hiring decisions at the same pace.

To me, this points to a selective market. A few years ago, many employers were willing to train someone, stretch on experience, or move quickly to secure talent. Today, companies still need people and positions are still being approved, but more organizations seem to be searching for the candidate who checks every box.

As recruiters, we’re seeing candidates passed over because they’re missing one certification, one software platform, or one specific industry background.

And that’s where I find myself asking a simple question:

Does the perfect candidate actually exist?

The perfect candidate to me is described as someone with the exact experience, exact industry background, exact technical skills, exact certifications, and the ability to contribute immediately with little to no ramp-up time.

The problem is that hiring rarely works that way, as people aren't robots.

Every week a position stays open impacts productivity, increases pressure on existing teams, delays projects, and can ultimately impact revenue. Waiting for perfection comes with a cost.

Meanwhile, recruiters are sorting through larger applicant pools than ever, trying to determine who can actually do the job versus who simply knows how to optimize a resume or application. That’s why today’s market feels so confusing.

Candidates see hundreds of applications leading nowhere and assume there are no jobs. Hiring managers feel like they aren’t seeing enough qualified talent. Recruiters are caught in the middle trying to fill positions that remain open longer than expected. As data from Pin, shows 60% of companies report longer times to fill requisitions.

Based on the data and the conversations I’m having across the industry, I don’t think the biggest challenge is a lack of talent.

I think many organizations are struggling to decide how much imperfection they’re willing to accept.

The perfect candidate is a comforting idea because it suggests there’s a risk-free hiring decision waiting to be made. The question is whether we’re overlooking great candidates while waiting to see if a perfect candidate exists.
