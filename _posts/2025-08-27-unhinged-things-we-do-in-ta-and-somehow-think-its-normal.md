Being in Talent Acquisition can be strange for many of us. We live in this odd intersection where business expectations, human emotions, and systems from 2004 collide daily. Somewhere along the way, we’ve normalized behaviors that, if you really think about them, are borderline unhinged.

Here is a few that I think are completely unhinged.

1\. Posting Jobs That Require “5 Years Experience” in a 2-Year-Old Technology

We’ve all seen this. A new software rolls out, becomes the next big thing, and suddenly the job description is demanding “5+ years of experience” with it. Never mind the fact that the tech didn’t exist five years ago.

2\. Sourcing Passive Candidates…Then Getting Mad They’re Passive

“Why aren’t they replying?”

Because, they already have a job and don't want to talk to a recruiter. You *sourced* them. You *literally* went and knocked on their digital door, but now you’re frustrated they didn’t open it fast enough?

It’s like fishing, getting a bite, and yelling at the fish for not jumping into the boat.

3\. Ghosting Candidates…But Expecting Candidates to Be Available ASAP

This one really grinds my gears. We’re all guilty of letting an application or email go dark for a week (or longer). But the moment a candidate doesn’t reply within 24 hours, it’s:

“Are they still interested? They’re not responsive.”

Yeah… pot, meet kettle.

4\. Putting “We’re Like a Family” in Job Posts (When It’s Actually a Dysfunctional One)

Every job post is about a “tight-knit family-like culture.” But deep down, we know this isn’t the Brady Bunch. It’s more like Thanksgiving dinner after someone brings up politics.

5\. Using ATS “Knockout” Questions That Knock Out Everyone

First, what year is it that we have knockout question. We all see questions, “Do you at least have Bachelor’s degree?” “Do you have 10+ years of experience?” “Are you willing to relocate to a city you have no interest in living and travel 7 days a week?"

Congratulations, you’ve filtered out 99% of potential candidates before even looking at a single resume.

But hey, you dispense all the candidates, right?

6\. Scheduling 6 Rounds of Interviews and Acting Like That’s Normal

It starts with a recruiter screen.

Then the hiring manager.

Then the panel.

Then the VP “just to see.”

Then the culture fit chat.

Then a “final” round. (Spoiler: It’s not final.)

Would you go through six dates before deciding to go get coffee with someone? No. But we expect candidates to do it for a job they haven’t even seen an offer for yet.

7\. Requiring a 3-Page Application After They Already Uploaded a Resum**e**

We *know* the resume has all the information.

We *know* the resume parsing doesn’t work right.

We *know* this is a terrible experience.

But does that stop us? Nope. “It’s the process.”

8\. Asking Candidates to “Bring Energy” or "Outgoing Personality" to an Interview…While Being on Mute 90% of the Call

The candidate is trying to impress. They’re smiling, engaging, giving their best self.

Meanwhile, we’re on mute, nodding into the void like a judgmental Zoom statue.

9\. Telling Laid Off Candidates to “Network More”

Networking is important. Laid off candidates have a tough road of applying to open roles. Still having a family at home. Also, now have no income.

It’s like giving someone a bucket and telling them to go find water… in the desert.

10\. Chasing “Cultural Fit” Then Wondering Why We Lack Diversity

“Does this candidate fit our culture?” is often code for “Do they make us feel comfortable because they’re similar to us?”

The irony? Then we hold a meeting about why we can’t seem to hire for diversity.

We navigate a minefield of processes, expectations, and contradictions. And while some of these unhinged behaviors have become “business as usual,” maybe it’s time we laugh about them, then fix a few along the way. But first, let’s get through that seventh round of interviews…
