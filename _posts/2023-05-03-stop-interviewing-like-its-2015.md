Job interviews have been an integral part of the hiring process for many years, but in recent times, there has been a shift towards a more candidate-centric approach. The traditional interview process of asking generic questions, evaluating responses, and assessing skills is no longer enough. Instead, companies should focus on interviewing with the candidate experience in mind. Here are some reasons why we should stop interviewing like it’s 2015 and start interviewing with the candidate experience in mind:

1\. Stop asking candidates to take entire days off work to go to your interview that could have been completed by zoom.

Asking candidates to take time off work to attend an interview can be a significant inconvenience. It can be especially challenging for those with existing commitments, such as caring for a family member or attending classes. By using video conferencing software such as Zoom, companies can conduct interviews remotely, reducing the amount of time candidates have to take off work.

2\. Stop using the thought process of "if they really want to work for our company, they will do this." Candidates have lots of options.

In the current job market, candidates have many options. As such, companies need to focus on creating a positive candidate experience. The interview process should be an opportunity to showcase the company's culture, values, and mission. If the process is cumbersome, time-consuming, or disrespectful of a candidate's time, they may choose to look elsewhere.

3\. Stop asking for a cumbersome application process.

The application process can be a significant barrier to entry for candidates. Companies should aim to make the process as streamlined as possible. This includes reducing the number of application steps, making the process mobile-friendly, and ensuring that the application is accessible to all candidates. By doing so, companies can reduce the risk of losing candidates due to a frustrating or cumbersome application process.

4\. Don't ask candidates to do projects that take hours to complete. Candidates have lives outside of work

Some companies ask candidates to complete projects as part of the interview process. While this can be an effective way to assess a candidate's skills, it can also be a significant time commitment. Companies should be mindful of the time that candidates are asked to dedicate to these projects. It is essential to strike a balance between assessing a candidate's skills and respecting their time.
