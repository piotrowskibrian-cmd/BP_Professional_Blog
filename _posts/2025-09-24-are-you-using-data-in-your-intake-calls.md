When it comes to intake calls with hiring managers, most recruiters show up ready to talk job descriptions, interviews, and timelines. But if you’re not walking in with real insights, market data, compensation benchmarks, geographic availability, competitor activity, and skill supply versus demand, what value are you actually bringing to the business?

Data isn’t just for dashboards and reports. It shapes your outcome and build trust immediately conversations. A former Formula 1 team principal, Otmar Szafnauer, said it best: “reasonable people with the same information will come to the same conclusion.” The assumption is that everyone is reasonable.

When you bring labor market insights, competitor benchmarks, compensation ranges, and talent pool data, you’re showing up prepared and ready to fill the role. It grounds the conversation in reality. We've all been in a room when a hiring manager wants a profile that doesn't exists or there is a very limited candidate pool. It also better for you and the manager to know that information immediately.  Hopefully, not just ready to post and pray.

Most hiring managers don’t hire every day. We do. That’s why showing up with numbers, trends, and real-time insight builds credibility fast. It turns you into a trusted advisor quickly.

What information should you bring? Well it depends what information you have on hand.   Got a dedicated insights tool? Perfect. If not, you can still find plenty:

How many resumes show up on Indeed, ZipRecruiter, or LinkedIn for that skill set?

How many competitors are currently hiring the same role?

How the available pool for a hard-to-fill role compares to something more common?

Compensation trends by geography or industry?

What percentage of candidates are actively applying versus just “open to work?”

None of it has to be fancy. It just has to be factual and real. One recruiter once told me they compared their role to HRBPs in the same market. Why? To show just how much harder their role would be to fill.

That’s the difference, you’re shaping how the business thinks about talent. Hard-to-fill role? Compare it to an easy one. Thin local market? Show them where the talent actually sits. Pay too low? Prove it with comp data.
