You’ve successfully made it to the interview phase—nice!

Now you're probably wondering... *how do I even start preparing?*

I got you.

Here’s my step-by-step guide to help you show up confident, clear, and ready to crush your next interview.

1\. Start with the Job Description

Break it down. Look at the core responsibilities, the technical and functional skills, and the behavioral traits they’re looking for.

Understand what the job is actually asking for—and be prepared to speak to each area in your experience. No one knows your background better than you. Own it. Connect the dots for them.

2\. Get Ready for Behavioral Questions

Yes, we are still doing behavioral interview questions.

Be prepared to talk about *how* you handle situations: conflict, feedback, failure, leadership, success.

Use real examples. Think STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Practice talking through your stories with clear outcomes.

3\. Prep for Role-Specific Questions

Think about the level you’re interviewing for.

- Individual Contributor? Expect scenario-based questions on ownership, execution, and how you work cross-functionally.
- Manager? You’ll be asked about your management style, conflict resolution, coaching, and decision-making. And please—*don’t just say “I’m fair and treat everyone equally.”* Be specific.
- Director? Get ready for questions on budgeting, strategy, team building, and cross-functional leadership.

The higher the level, the more they want to know how you think, influence, and drive results through others.

4\. Use ChatGPT as Your Interview Coach

You’re literally talking to a tool that can help you prep right now. Here's how to use it:

- Drop in a potential interview question.
- Practice your answer out loud.
- Use voice or text, and ask for feedback.

You’ll get instant suggestions on how to polish your response, improve structure, and show more impact. It’s like having a personal coach—on demand.

5\. Always Ask Questions

This is huge. Interviewers *notice* when candidates come in curious and engaged.

Prep a few solid questions in advance. And if they get answered during the conversation? No problem—ask about the interviewer:

- Why did you join the company?
- What’s kept you there?
- What keeps them up about work?
- What does success look like in this role?
- What are the biggest challenges ahead?
- What does growth look like on this team?

Questions like these show you’re not just here to *get* the job—you’re here to *understand* the opportunity.

Before You Walk In: Mindset Matters

This part’s underrated, but it makes a difference.

Go in knowing:

- You’re not just being evaluated—you’re evaluating them, too.
- You know your value. You know your story. You’ve done the work.
- The goal isn’t to be perfect—it’s to be real, prepared, and present.

Confidence shifts everything.
