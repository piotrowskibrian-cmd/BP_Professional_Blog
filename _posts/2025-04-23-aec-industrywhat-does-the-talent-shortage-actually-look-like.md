AEC industry—we hearing about how we’re facing a shortage of engineers and architects, but we’re not looking closely enough at the numbers and where we’re actually heading.

We focus on what the shortage looks like today or maybe the next couple years, but rarely do we talk about the underlying trend. So let’s lay out the data, keep it real, and give you everything you need to see the bigger picture. Every stat here has a source—go check it for yourself.

Right now, 25% of the AEC workforce is over 55 years old. (Source: Autodesk Design & Make Report 2024) If that sounds right, it’s probably because when you look around your office, your clients, or partners, you see it happening. And let’s be clear—this isn’t about ageism. It’s about experience and the knowledge we will be losing to retirement.

Here’s what we’re seeing every single year:

- 2–3% of the AEC workforce retires, which doesn’t seem bad—until you realize that 25% of the workforce will be at retirement age within 10 years.  
(Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, BuildForce Canada)
- Another 2–3% leaves for other industries  
(Sources: Enginuity Advantage, COPS Canada)
- And that doesn’t even count people leaving for other firms

Now layer on this: about 33% of open roles in the AEC industry don’t get filled.  
(Source: ASCE, Autodesk) What happens to that work? It gets passed to someone with already a full plate of work leading to overworked teams, missed deadlines, and burnout.

*What Does it Look Like right now?*

Here’s a breakdown from the two markets I focus on: the U.S. and Canada.

United States (Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics)

- Civil Engineers (includes structural engineers): 355,000+
- Mechanical Engineers (not all in AEC): 286,000
- Electrical Engineers (not all in AEC): 188,000
- Architects: 121,000
- Projected Growth through 2033:
  - Civil: 6%
  - Mechanical: 11%
- Estimated annual new entrants:
  - Civil: ~18,000
  - Mechanical: ~15,000
  - Electrical: ~12,000
  - Architects: ~4,000

Canada (Sources: ESDC / Job Bank Canada, Engineers Canada, ACSA):

- Civil Engineers (includes structural engineers): 124,200
- Mechanical Engineers (not all in AEC): 113,100
- Electrical Engineers (not all in AEC): 28,500
- Architects: 7,000
- Estimated annual job openings (2024–2033):
  - Civil: ~6,830
  - Mechanical: ~4,690
  - Electrical: ~1,280
  - Architects: ~200
- Estimated annual new people to the market.
  - Engineering Degrees Awarded: ~18,185 (latest data from Engineers Canada)
  - Architecture Students Enrolled in M.Arch programs: ~1,200 (ACSA)

*Note: Degree and enrollment numbers reflect educational pipelines, not direct workforce entry, but they give a directional sense of talent inflow.*

But here’s the thing—on paper, the numbers don’t look that bad. Until you zoom out.

We’re seeing thousands of new grads, sure—but that doesn’t mean they’re licensed, experienced, or even choosing careers in building design. Many won’t ever work in civil infrastructure, HVAC design, or large-scale architecture. And over the next 10 years, we’re on track to lose a quarter to a third of our current workforce. That’s senior-level talent, team leads, and SMEs—gone. And we don’t have a clean pipeline to replace them.

That’s where we are. I’m not here (yet) to offer solutions. That’s coming in the next post.

But if we don’t start with the facts, we’ll never have the right conversation. This is the state of the market right now. Pay attention to it.
